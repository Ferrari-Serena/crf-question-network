How to read this graph

1. Each color = one question cluster (7 clusters, algorithmically grouped)

Colors are not preset topics — Louvain community detection groups questions by how they link. Each of the 7 clusters addresses one issue:

Blue · Incentive TransmissionHow incentives cascade from institution → instructor → TA (promotion, KE policy, TA load)
Orange · Firm Motivation · Course DeliveryWhy firms enter the classroom + how the course is delivered (co-teaching / PBL / real projects)
Red · Sustainability · AttritionWill firms return next year, why they leave, time mismatch
Purple · Talent TypesWhat kind of talent firms want (screening / cost-sharing / shaping specs)
Green · Mechanism Design · GovernanceHow to align the five parties' incentives, split value, prevent firm capture
Yellow · Signaling · ScreeningStudents pay out of pocket for long-term returns; firms "try before they buy"
Cyan · Urgency · FairnessWho needs the cooperation more, each party's "currency", fairness

2. A line between two points = "these two questions share the same root"

A line is not "this question causes that one" (not causal), but "these two are two sides of the same thing". Composed of two signals:

  • Shared tags (60%): same Nobel theory / same citation / same cooperation mode / same governance dimension
  • Shared wording (40%): semantic similarity of English wording (TF-IDF)

Hover or click any node — the right panel lists "why linked (shared tags / citations)", which is the evidence for that line.

3. Why some clusters mix together

Red (attrition), purple (talent types), green (mechanism design) sit on the same "firm incentive × governance" territory — attrition is the symptom, types the cause, mechanism design the cure; they are semantically adjacent, so they naturally clump. This is not a rendering bug but a real finding: these three issues are inherently hard to separate.

4. What the network says (one line)

The central questions fall on "incentive alignment + sustainability", not any single stakeholder — confirming that "four forces into one" mechanism design is the main research axis.
